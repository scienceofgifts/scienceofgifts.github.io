An alien visiting our planet, presumably who understands our language, would be baffled by the way we communicate. We don’t see it because we’re so used to it but human communication is complex in our use of subtext, non-verbal cues and other social gestures. Look at any autistic person trying to interpret us and you’ll see that we’re the weird ones. 

It takes us decades of social conditioning and experience to learn how to interpret these cues. It’s not surprising that almost 70% of our communication is non-verbal.

For the uninitiated, the gift exchange ritual that humans across all different cultures throughout history have partaken in, may seem like a straightforward exchange of goods. But if you look a little closer you’d see a lot going on under the hood. 

Gifts speak a language of their own. What you give and how you give conveys meaning to the recipient. This has important social implications. It’s necessary to understand this language to avoid mistakes that could harm your relationships. 


## Gifts as Signals {#H1}
<div class="right-image">
    <img itemprop="image" src="../assets/woman-giving-gift-graphic.jpg" loading="lazy" width="400" height="400" alt="Woman with a gift"/>
    <span class="caption"> 
       When a gift is more than just a gift.   
    </span>
</div>

Sociologists and Anthropologists who have studied this subject mostly have a view that gifts convey meaning and send signals. 

Following are some signals that we send:

1. Time, Money and Effort Invested.
2. Social Status
3. Knowledge of the Recipient’s Interests
4. Selflessness
5. Narcissism
6. Adherence to the group
7. Insecurities


{% include research.html %}

> A study on the role of gifts in romantic relationships concluded that for both men and women, using gifts to announce relationships is more likely to decrease the chances of a breakup. The researchers warn that gifts should only be used to make good relationships endure and not to turn a poor relationship into a successful one.<sup>[4]</sup> 

A gift also **signals how much time, effort and money you’ve put into picking it**. This is because the more you care about someone and value your relationship with them, the more likely you are to invest those things into buying a gift to ensure the relationship is maintained. 

Another important **signal is selflessness.** People often spend money on gifts that don’t provide much in terms of value to the recipient. There’s a wastefulness associated with spending money on gifts. 

Research suggests that recipients tend to prefer gifts that are practical.<sup>[5]</sup> So, have people been doing it wrong all this while? Turns out, no. There’s a reason gift stores are filled with things that aren’t of much use outside of being gifts. The inefficiency is on purpose. Such a gift signals the willingness to sacrifice money for the other person, since they know that they’ll get an equally inefficient gift in return. This proves that the exchange was a symbolic gesture rather than a way to get monetary gain. 

This helps you root out the potential “gold diggers.” The recipient, by being glad to receive an inefficient gift, signals that they aren’t one.

## 1. Time, Money and Effort Invested {#H2}

<div class="right-image">
    <img itemprop="image" src="../assets/gift-card-graphic.jpg" loading="lazy" width="400" height="400" alt="Gift Card Graphic"/>
    <span class="caption"> 
       Cash gifts are inefficient signals.     
    </span>
</div>

There’s a rule of human nature. You invest in people you care about and you care about people you invest in. The more you do for someone, the more sacrifice, in terms of time, effort and money, the more you come to like them. This is why people slip into one-sided relationships where they try to win over people they like by doing things for them but their feelings are not reciprocated. The trope of the friendzoned guy is a good example. This is, of course, an unhealthy way to have a relationship. 

When it comes to gifts, you’re going to put in efforts into figuring out what they want if you care about them liking their gift. Alternatively, if you’re not invested in a person, you’re less likely to waste much effort or time doing it. 

A good gift signals to the recipient that you have made the investment to maintain the relationship.

> Make sure you consider your recipient’s interests and needs when picking a gift for them. Oftentimes, they’ll subtly express their wishes to you. Also, put some effort into the presentation.

## 2. Closeness of The Relationship {#H3}


A gift can be a test of true friendship or of chemistry in romantic relationships. It may sound irrelevant in the big scheme of things. After all, there are more important things that matter, but it’s not a trivial matter. 

Picking a gift that matches the recipient’s interests proves to the recipient that you’re close enough to know of their interests. It’s a filter of sorts. It’s the same as how someone having a college degree signals to others that they’re smart enough to obtain a college degree. They wouldn’t be able to if they weren’t. The same logic applies here. Only people who are close enough to you to know will be able to guess what you want. 

It’s not even about getting it 100% correct, since you’re the only person who knows about your own needs and wants at any time. It’s about the attempt. If they know that you’re a history buff who loves collecting war memorabilia and they gift you a rare item you’ve been looking for, this passes the test. 

This is why referencing wish lists or cash gifts are ineffective in close relationships. They don’t signal much of anything about your knowledge of the recipient's interests. 

Note: Sometimes recipients express their desire for a particular gift or hint at their wishes in less obvious ways by making subtle suggestions when potential givers are around. You can pass the test if you’re observant and paying attention to this. 


{% include tipbox-2.html %}

> When buying a gift for a close friend, try to pick something based on information about them that only you are privy to. It could be an interest that many people don’t know about or a wish they had, such as a concert they wanted to go to.

## Summary {#H4}


Gifts are signals. They signal efforts you put into picking them, how much you know the person and your willingness to sacrifice to maintain the relationship. This can’t be achieved with cash gifts. 

It’s appropriate to give cash & gift cards either when you don’t have a close relationship with your recipient or when they’re not financially independent and could use the money.
