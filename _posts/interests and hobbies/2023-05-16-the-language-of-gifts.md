An alien visiting our planet, presumably who understands our language, would be baffled by the way we communicate. We don’t see it because we’re so used to it but human communication is complex in our use of subtext, non-verbal cues and other social gestures. Look at any autistic person trying to interpret us and you’ll see that we’re the weird ones. 

It takes us decades of social conditioning and experience to learn how to interpret these cues. It’s not surprising that almost 70% of our communication is non-verbal.

For the uninitiated, the gift exchange ritual that humans across all different cultures throughout history have partaken in, may seem like a straightforward exchange of goods. But if you look a little closer you’d see a lot going on under the hood. 

Gifts speak a language of their own. What you give and how you give conveys meaning to the recipient. This has important social implications. It’s necessary to understand this language to avoid mistakes that could harm your relationships. 


## Gifts as Signals {#H1}
<div class="right-image">
    <img itemprop="image" src="../assets/gifts-as-signals-graphic.jpg" loading="lazy" width="400" height="400" alt="Woman with a gift"/>
    <span class="caption"> 
       When a gift is more than just a gift.   
    </span>
</div>

Sociologists and Anthropologists who have studied this subject mostly have a view that gifts convey meaning and send signals. 

Following are some signals that we send:

1. Investment in The Relationship
2. Social Status
3. Knowledge of the Recipient’s Interests
4. Selflessness
5. Narcissism
6. Adherence to the group
7. Insecurities


{% include research.html %}

> A study on the role of gifts in romantic relationships concluded that for both men and women, using gifts to announce relationships is more likely to decrease the chances of a breakup. The researchers warn that gifts should only be used to make good relationships endure and not to turn a poor relationship into a successful one.<sup>[4]</sup> 

A gift also **signals how much time, effort and money you’ve put into picking it**. This is because the more you care about someone and value your relationship with them, the more likely you are to invest those things into buying a gift to ensure the relationship is maintained. 

Another important **signal is selflessness.** People often spend money on gifts that don’t provide much in terms of value to the recipient. There’s a wastefulness associated with spending money on gifts. 

Research suggests that recipients tend to prefer gifts that are practical.<sup>[5]</sup> So, have people been doing it wrong all this while? Turns out, no. There’s a reason gift stores are filled with things that aren’t of much use outside of being gifts. The inefficiency is on purpose. Such a gift signals the willingness to sacrifice money for the other person, since they know that they’ll get an equally inefficient gift in return. This proves that the exchange was a symbolic gesture rather than a way to get monetary gain. 

This helps you root out the potential “gold diggers.” The recipient, by being glad to receive an inefficient gift, signals that they aren’t one.

## 1. How Invested You Are in The Relationship {#H2}

<div class="right-image">
    <img itemprop="image" src="../assets/invested-in-relationship-graphic.jpg" loading="lazy" width="400" height="400" alt="Gift Card Graphic"/>
    <span class="caption"> 
       Cash gifts are inefficient signals.     
    </span>
</div>

There’s a rule of human nature. You invest in people you care about and you care about people you invest in. The more you do for someone, the more sacrifice, in terms of time, effort and money, the more you come to like them. This is why people slip into one-sided relationships where they try to win over people they like by doing things for them but their feelings are not reciprocated. The trope of the friendzoned guy is a good example. This is, of course, an unhealthy way to have a relationship. 

When it comes to gifts, you’re going to put in efforts into figuring out what they want if you care about them liking their gift. Alternatively, if you’re not invested in a person, you’re less likely to waste much effort or time doing it. 

A good gift signals to the recipient that you have made the investment to maintain the relationship.  

{% include tipbox-2.html %}

> Make sure you consider your recipient’s interests and needs when picking a gift for them. Oftentimes, they’ll subtly express their wishes to you. Also, put some effort into the presentation.

## 2. How Close You Are With The Recipient {#H3}

<div class="right-image">
    <img itemprop="image" src="../assets/close-friends-graphic.jpg" loading="lazy" width="400" height="400" alt="Gift Card Graphic"/>
    <span class="caption"> 
       Cash gifts are inefficient signals.     
    </span>
</div>


A gift can be a test of true friendship or of chemistry in romantic relationships. It may sound irrelevant in the big scheme of things. After all, there are more important things that matter, but it’s not a trivial matter. 

Picking a gift that matches the recipient’s interests proves to the recipient that you’re close enough to know of their interests. It’s a filter of sorts. It’s the same as how someone having a college degree signals to others that they’re smart enough to obtain a college degree. They wouldn’t be able to if they weren’t. The same logic applies here. Only people who are close enough to you to know will be able to guess what you want. 

{% include example.html %}

> Make sure you consider your recipient’s interests and needs when picking a gift for them. Oftentimes, they’ll subtly express their wishes to you. Also, put some effort into the presentation. 

It’s not even about getting it 100% correct, since you’re the only person who knows about your own needs and wants at any time. It’s about the attempt. If they know that you’re a history buff who loves collecting war memorabilia and they gift you a rare item you’ve been looking for, this passes the test. 

This is why referencing wish lists or cash gifts are ineffective in close relationships. They don’t signal much of anything about your knowledge of the recipient's interests. 

Note: Sometimes recipients express their desire for a particular gift or hint at their wishes in less obvious ways by making subtle suggestions when potential givers are around. You can pass the test if you’re observant and paying attention to this. 


{% include tipbox-2.html %}

> When buying a gift for a close friend, try to pick something based on information about them that only you are privy to. It could be an interest that many people don’t know about or a wish they had, such as a concert they wanted to go to.

## 3. Your Social Status {#H4}

<div class="right-image">
    <img itemprop="image" src="../assets/your-social-status-graphic.jpg" loading="lazy" width="400" height="400" alt="Gift Card Graphic"/>
    <span class="caption"> 
       Cash gifts are inefficient signals.     
    </span>
</div>


People use gifts to enforce and maintain social hierarchies. This can be harmless or malicious depending on the intentions of the giver. People tend to give more expensive gifts to the wealthy people they like. They’re motivated by their desire to signal their own social status and are overly concerned about the wealthy recipient’s reaction to the gift. This behavior may stem from insecurities of the giver about their own status. 

Gift givers and recipients try to achieve a balanced reciprocity. To do this they reverse their roles through time  in order to maintain the exchange partnership. 

When you receive a gift from someone it creates tension as  the acceptance of the gift is an implicit recognition of dependance on the giver. A gift must be repaid or the failure will result in a loss of status and self esteem. There’s a sense of indebtedness that guides the gift exchange system. This is why it’s important to repay your gifts. 

Some people use this to “put people in their place” by giving them expensive gifts they know won’t be repaid. This is meant to put the recipient in an obligation and make them feel less. 

There’s an unwritten rule about giving cash gifts to someone. The giver should be older or of higher status than the recipient. Elders in a community tend to outmatch the value of gifts by the younger members.

Don’t give cash gifts to peers as it signals that you think of them as of lower status. 


{% include tipbox-2.html %}

> Alway give high quality products. This doesn’t mean expensive, just make sure they don’t come off as cheap. Also, match the value of the gifts you receive.

## 4. Selfishness {#H5}

<div class="right-image">
    <img itemprop="image" src="../assets/selfish-graphic.jpg" loading="lazy" width="400" height="400" alt="Gift Card Graphic"/>
    <span class="caption"> 
       Cash gifts are inefficient signals.     
    </span>
</div>

Gift selection by givers is mostly based on the giver's self concept and has less to do with the characteristics of the recipient. What you choose to give says a lot about you. 

Your gift giving behavior will reflect on your personality as a whole as it is driven by your motivations. 

Americans waste a lot of money on gifts. Exchanging gifts is a pretty wastfull tradition overall. Most people will give gifts that the recipients don’t desire or need. There’s a reason gift shops are full of items that are inefficient. 

There’s a method to this madness. Giving an inefficient gift which the recipient won’t have much utility for implies that you’ll get something similar in return. This means both you and your recipient have sunk money into your gifts. This sacrifice of money indicates that you care about your relationship with them enough to do it. It signals a sense of selflessness that is an important indicator of a successful relationship. 

A person who is unwilling to sacrifice money will likely be also unable to share overall. This inability to share indicates that this person can’t be trusted since they’ll always put their own needs over others. 


{% include tipbox-2.html %}

> Don’t give people gifts that are too practical unless they specifically ask for it. Gifts are meant to be sacrificial. And if you feel too reluctant to waste money on a gift for someone, that relationship may not be worth it or vice versa.

## 5. Narcissism {#H6}

<div class="right-image">
    <img itemprop="image" src="../assets/narcissist-graphic.jpg" loading="lazy" width="400" height="400" alt="Gift Card Graphic"/>

Not every gift is given with pure intentions. Gifts are signs of love and intimacy intended to please the recipient but they may also be self-serving in nature. Some people give not to please others rather to fulfill their own satisfactions. 

While egoism drives some gift giving behavior in everyone, for a narcissist it is the primary motivation. 

Narcissists report high maintenance motivations for gift giving. They were also driven by power motivation. They consider gift giving to be a tool for relationship maintenance or impression management. 


{% include tipbox-2.html %}

> Think about the recipient’s needs when picking a gift rather. Make it less about you and more about them. And if it feels like someone is giving you things to fulfill their own needs, they might be narcissistic and not to be relied on.

## 6. Whether You’re a Team Player {#H7}

<div class="right-image">
    <img itemprop="image" src="../assets/team-player-graphic.jpg" loading="lazy" width="400" height="400" alt="Gift Card Graphic"/>
    <span class="caption"> 
       Cash gifts are inefficient signals.

</div>

Gift exchange is a social activity confirming relationships and social interactions. In the context of gift exchange between members of a community, there are three themes that govern it. Shared rituals and traditions, values and responsibilities.

We learn social norms through experience and interacting with others. There are some unwritten rules that are passed down by parents to their children. 

In order to maintain their identity as a group member, they must adhere to accepted norms within the group. 

Social norms play an important role in gift giving communities. Going outside the boundaries of the expected community norms could potentially cause problems within group members. 

There’s a shared understanding of an appropriate gift, the right timing and how the gift should be presented. When givers stick to these unwritten rules, their gifts are considered thoughtful and they are considered to be committed to the community. 

People don’t want to be embarrassed within their community by giving cheap gifts or items that could not be returned. 

This behavior can be observed in families that have their own gift-giving rituals and norms. You can have a different set of rituals among other groups you’re a part of such as with close friends and coworkers, etc. 



{% include tipbox-2.html %}

> Stick to your group's social norms if you want to fit in. If you’re a new member, find clues about the rules based on the history of gift exchange among others.

## 7. Insecurities {#H8}

<div class="right-image">
    <img itemprop="image" src="../assets/insecure-graphic.jpg" loading="lazy" width="400" height="400" alt="Gift Card Graphic"/>
    <span class="caption"> 
       Cash gifts are inefficient signals.     
    </span>
</div>

No one can honestly consider themselves completely secure. Everyone has insecurities. That’s just the nature of living in a world full of uncertainties and dangers. Having self doubt once in a while can be healthy. After all, only a true narcissist is devoid of it. 

Insecurities become problematic when it affects our lives or the lives of others in a negative way. If you’re insecure about your relationship, that’ll manifest in different forms, such as being overly suspicious or over controlling of our partners. This stems from a belief that your partner is too good for you or you’re not worthy of their love. 

This reflects in gift giving behavior too. If your partner is giving you gifts that are too expensive or buys you gifts too often, that’s likely a sign of this stemming from their insecurities about your relationship with them. Especially if you don’t return the favor. And while giving a little too much to people you love isn’t always unhealthy, there’s a problem when you cross the line. How much is too much? I’ll say it’s when it starts to make the recipient uncomfortable. 

If you’re in a romantic relationship and your partner buys you a very expensive gift too early in the relationship, that could be a sign that they don’t feel secure enough and likely feel the need to “buy” your love. This doesn’t have to be intentional though. Their insecurities will just guide them.  


{% include tipbox-2.html %}

> Avoid giving too many gifts to your partner. You’ll come off as needy. And if you feel the need to do so, reflect on if that is coming from some kind of insecurity. If your partner buys you too many gifts, talk to them and address their insecurities.

## Summary {#H4}


Gifts convey meaning. What you choose to give tells your recipient how you feel about your relationship with them. Your choice reflects how much time, effort and money you spend on the gift, signaling investment.

Among close friends, exchanging gifts is a test that tells the recipient how well you know them. Gifts also signal your social status within a group. 

There’s an inherent wastefulness in the nature of gift exchange. Not willing to “waste” money by opting for practical gifts indicates selfishness, making you seem less reliable. 

If you spend too much on gifts for your partner, it’s likely a sign of a deeper issue with insecurities. Also, it comes off as you trying to buy them and makes you seem needy.
